Independent audit of Fractalus up38
Date: 2026-10-27
English: AUDIT_REPORT.txt | Polski: AUDIT_REPORT_PL.txt

Conclusion
The cartridge speedup is credible and reproducible. In the examined cases,
the changes preserve the image and the behavior of the tested routines.
No confirmed game defect was found. This is not proof of correctness for
every state of the entire game, particularly the additional XEX relocation.

Meaning of the changes
1. wait_beam allows clearing after ANTIC has left the mountain area, without
   waiting for VBI itself. This relaxes our conservative synchronization
   condition; it does not unconditionally remove the wait.
2. raster_cull extends our conservative E(w) bound to internal subtrees.
   It does not reduce visible terrain subdivision depth. Reading up to three
   extra columns can prevent culling, not incorrectly force it.
3. VBI skips initial inactive sound channels faster and unrolls the cockpit
   gauge. The main active-sound paths remain.
4. sky retains the clean-sky boundary for each byte column. Clearing and
   filling perform fewer stores. This is a substantial algorithmic change
   that depends on every drawing routine updating the boundaries correctly.
5. proj_loop uses banking, inlined division, a revised edge loop, fewer copies,
   and unrolled prolog/epilog code. No approximation or resolution reduction
   was identified; completed images were compared against the reference.

Independent correctness tests
- 320 clear/render/fill states captured from up38: PAL/NTSC, neutral flight
  and steering/firing, with additional level-selection input sequences.
  Images and compared RAM match, retaining known exclusions for dead rasterizer
  variables, private sky state, and dead stack memory.
- 20 states returned different C/V flags after the pipeline. Traced subsequent
  execution: each differing flag was overwritten before being read. These
  differences were not dismissed merely because the images matched.
- 2500 random segments for the isolated raster_cull patch: matching pixels
  and occlusion profiles, including reversed and offscreen endpoints.
  Flags after the individual routine do not always match; this does not claim
  complete register equivalence for every arbitrary routine invocation.
- 320 VBI states: matching compared RAM and hardware-write sequences, excluding
  bank switches. Registers and flags match after XITVBV/RTI. Register differences
  before the OS exit were intermediate state.
- 300 object-pixel calls, covering both $8B80 and $8B88 entries. During rendering,
  compared equivalent representations: retained clean sky in up38 corresponds
  to zeroes in the reference buffer before filling. After this normalization,
  compared RAM and A/X/Y/SP match. This tests the internal representation;
  it does not claim memory must be identical before image completion.
- 100 PAL and 100 NTSC frames: sampled successive rows after ANTIC fetches,
  with no changes within a period displaying the same buffer. Row 46, updated
  by VBI, was excluded as specified by the test. No tearing detected.
- 6 configurations: CAR 64K, XEX 320k, XEX 1088k, each in PAL/NTSC. Each passed
  3 Esc pause/resume cycles, 5 successive images after each resume, steering,
  and firing. Inspected control screenshots from PAL CAR/XEX.

Important limitations and documentation observations
- Not all landing, pilot rescue, alien, game-over, and new-game scenes were
  dynamically verified. This matters particularly for XEX, relocated partly
  using code coverage and manual decisions.
- No physical Atari, cartridge-device, or accelerator tests. The README's
  supported cartridge list is not backed by hardware tests in this package.
- The authors explicitly acknowledge XEX limitations; retain that disclosure.
- equiv.py excludes the entire stack page, $B5, and a list of dead variables;
  it does not compare P flags. It does not emulate DMA or interrupts itself.
  'All RAM identical' needs these qualifications. Our additional tests covered
  live stack memory, VBI exit, and flags separately.
- AGENT.md describes hang.py as random steering and firing, but that script
  performs neutral flight. Other tools, including capture.py, do support
  steering. This does not prove those tests were absent; the description of
  the particular script is inaccurate.
- The authors' measurement lacks our $4A-change watchpoint and checks mode at
  successive stops. It is not literally identical measurement code. Eight
  replications with our code, including an early end, matched.
- VBI timing changes alter RANDOM reads and generated planets. Image equality
  refers to identical algorithm inputs, not an identical movie after the same
  menu delay. Full-series comparisons should remain independent; ladder steps
  are not exact valuations of isolated patches on identical scenes, and their
  percentages must not be added.
- The gain applies to the defined automatic flight. Our clear/render/fill
  microtests found: PAL neutral -10.37% cycles; NTSC neutral -10.06%; NTSC
  steering -6.11%; PAL steering +1.96%. The last sample was slightly slower
  in this subsystem. This is neither full-game FPS nor evidence of an overall
  regression; it shows that cache value depends on the scene. Not every image
  must become faster.
- One energy-routine frequency measurement does not establish equivalence of
  every game mechanism. A universal 25 Hz logic-clock claim is too broad
  (TV standard and different execution paths also matter). No deliberate
  gameplay acceleration, enemy omission, or LOD reduction was identified.

Assessment
A valuable, genuine continuation of optimization; the earlier +19% was not
the limit. For the cartridge: strong evidence of speedup and correctness
of the tested rendering, no discovered defect, without an absolute guarantee
for the entire game. For XEX: working and reproducible, with a separate level
of relocation risk; it still needs rare-scene and physical-hardware testing.
